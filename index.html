Questions, answered

Questions before
you get in touch.

The questions people often have before working with a sales closer. Answered straight, the way I'd answer them in conversation.

How do you approach a sales conversation?+

I start by listening. I want to understand what the prospect needs, then I explain relevant solutions clearly and honestly, so they can make a decision they feel confident about.

Can you work with my product or offer?+

I take the time to understand an offer properly before speaking to prospects about it. If something is unclear, I would rather ask questions than assume.

What happens when a prospect needs time to think?+

That is a reasonable response. I ask whether there are any questions I can answer, give honest information, and agree on a sensible next step so the prospect never feels rushed.

How do you handle price questions?+

I explain clearly what is included and how the solution relates to the prospect's needs, so they understand what they are deciding on. Honest, plain explanations come first.

How do we agree on working terms?+

Working arrangements are discussed directly, so both sides are clear on expectations before starting. Get in touch and we can have that conversation.

What if a prospect isn't ready to buy?+

I respect their decision. A prospect who is not ready should never be pressured, and keeping the relationship professional and respectful leaves the door open for the future.

Process

Understanding the Prospect

Discovery Step 1 Sales process

Every good sales conversation starts with understanding what the person on the other side actually needs.

Quick facts

  • Role Sales Closer
  • Stage Opening conversation
  • Focus Needs and questions
  • Style Listen first

The goal

Before recommending anything, I want to understand the prospect's situation, priorities, and concerns. A recommendation only makes sense once the need is clear.

How I approach it

  • Ask open questions that let the prospect explain what they are looking for in their own words.
  • Listen attentively, without rushing to a solution.
  • Confirm my understanding, so nothing important is assumed.
  • Be honest about whether a solution is genuinely relevant to the prospect's needs.

Why it matters

A prospect who feels understood is better placed to make a confident decision, and the conversation that follows is more honest for both sides.

Process

Explaining the Solution

Conversation Step 2 Sales process

Once the need is clear, the next job is to explain how a solution fits it, plainly and honestly.

Quick facts

  • Role Sales Closer
  • Stage Solution conversation
  • Focus Clarity
  • Style Plain and honest

The goal

The prospect should leave the conversation knowing exactly what is being offered, how it relates to what they told me, and what it means for them.

How I approach it

  • Explain the solution in plain language, without jargon or pressure.
  • Connect each point back to what the prospect has shared about their needs.
  • Answer questions directly, including being clear about what a solution does not cover.
  • Leave room for the prospect to think and decide.

Why it matters

Clear explanations remove guesswork. When people understand what they are deciding on, they decide with more confidence and trust.

Process

Guiding the Next Step

Follow-up Step 3 Sales process

A good conversation ends with the prospect knowing exactly what happens next.

Quick facts

  • Role Sales Closer
  • Stage Decision and follow-up
  • Focus Next steps
  • Style Professional and respectful

The goal

To help the prospect move forward at a pace that suits them, with a clear understanding of the process and what is expected at each stage.

How I approach it

  • Summarise what has been discussed, so everyone is working from the same understanding.
  • Explain the next steps in the sales process clearly.
  • Follow up professionally, without being pushy.
  • Respect the prospect's decision, whatever it is.

Why it matters

Prospects often lose momentum simply because the next step was unclear. Consistent, courteous follow-up keeps the conversation moving and the relationship strong.

Finding them later

In Gmail, search "Website enquiry" to pull up every one of them in one list, oldest to newest. If you want them easier to spot, create a filter for that subject line and have Gmail label them automatically, so they never get lost in the rest of your inbox.

If you would rather have a running list instead
